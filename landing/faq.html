FAQ

Short answers about mapping, hunting, accounts and what the map shows.

What is Mesh-Hunter?

A mapping and node-hunting toolkit for MeshCore. The RX webapp logs every packet your companion radio hears, placed with your phone's GPS. The shared map shows every mapper's receptions together, and is where you follow one node's signal to where it transmits from.

What do I need to start mapping?

A MeshCore companion radio, and a phone with GPS and a browser that supports Web Bluetooth, such as Chrome on Android or the Bluefy browser on iOS. Open the RX webapp, pair the radio over Bluetooth, and walk or drive. The webapp installs as a PWA: add it to your home screen.

Do I need an account?

Not to read the map: without one you see all-time coarse coverage and the last 24 hours of individual receptions. An account shows your own receptions in full detail. It is made in the RX webapp, from the companion you paired, which is why the map itself has no sign-up form. Member verification, granted by the admins, opens the full history, Locate and the CoreScope layers.

Does the map show where my node is?

No. Position is inferred from radio measurements (RSSI/SNR) via mesh topology, not from GPS tracking of any target node. The coordinates stored with each reception are the mapping phone's own position at the moment of reception. The map shows where a mapper was when they heard your node and how well, not where your node stands.

Verified members can run Locate, which works the other way: it estimates where a node transmits from out of the readings around it. That is still an inference from radio measurements rather than a position your node reports, and it lands a few hundred metres out on average, so it is a search area and not a fix.

Who sees what my radio hears?

Every reception you map is published to the shared map. Visitors without an account see it coarse and recent, you see your own receptions in full detail, and verified members see everything.

Does it cost anything?

No. Mesh-Hunter is run by amateur-radio operators; accounts and member verification are free.

Which nodes can I hunt?

Any node that transmits: a companion, a repeater, a sensor or a room server. Hunting means driving toward where its signal is heard strongest, so anything that transmits can be found.

How does Mesh-Hunter relate to the other community tools?

Mesh-Hunter builds on CoreScope, the mesh observatory by ON8AR. The Community section on the front page links the wider ecosystem: the Dutch MeshCore Community, the DMC Triangulator, MC Radar and MeshWiki.
