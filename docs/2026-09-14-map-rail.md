# The map's controls are one FAB rail, the app's (#630)

**Date:** 2026-09-14
**Status:** decided (Kasper, 2026-09-13 and 14, on #630), implemented
**Related:** #630, #643 (the ticker pinned under the bar on a phone), #653 (node positions refused visibly to a guest, #629/#631/#641), #595 (the 2D/3D button), #603 (the three node-position stops), `docs/2026-09-06-map-in-3d.md`, `docs/2026-09-08-coverage-overview.md`

## What changed

The map's corner held two control boxes: MapLibre's NavigationControl (zoom and compass) and the 2D/3D button `mapcore.js addButton` put beside it as a second control group. Node positions was a segmented control in the filter panel. The app has one column of round buttons for the same jobs.

Now the map has that column too, `#map-rail` in `web/index.html`, wired in `web/map.js` from the pure decisions in `web/maprail.js`:

1. **One rail, bottom right, at every width.** Zoom in, zoom out, compass, 2D/3D (`#view-toggle`, its `aria-pressed` and its `View: 2D` / `View: 3D` labels kept) and node positions (`#nodepos-toggle`), top to bottom. 46px round buttons at `right: 14px`, 8px apart, on `--ch-surface`, the accent ring outside the button when on, the app's disabled look. Labels only, no `title`, as the app's FABs and the old view button. z-index 624: over the ticker (620), under the filter panel's scrim (626).
2. **MapLibre's NavigationControl is removed, not restyled**, and `addButton` went with it: the view button is static markup in the rail. The AttributionControl stays at the bottom right, and the rail stands clear above it (44px up, 64px on a phone, where the credit can wrap to two lines).
3. **The compass turns the map to north and nothing else.** Pitch is kept, because flattening a 3D view is the view button's job. No drag on the compass. The needle turns with the bearing and does not tilt with the pitch, since a press does not change the pitch. The reset eases unless the reader prefers reduced motion (`northResetEase`).
4. **Zoom buttons are disabled at the map's exact min and max zoom**, repainted on every `zoom` (`zoomButtonsDisabled`), so a press that would do nothing is not offered.
5. **Node positions moved out of the filter panel** to the rail, as the app's FAB: the app's icon, its three stops (off, positions, reach) and its segmented ring (`fabring.js`). `nodeposmode.js` and `fabring.js` are the app's files whole, pinned byte for byte in `web/parity.test.js`, and `?nodepos=` takes the app's vocabulary: `positions` or `reach`, absent when off. An old `?nodepos=1` link now lands on off, which is accepted for a product in development.
6. **A guest's tap explains instead of switching.** Below member the button is visible and enabled; a tap keeps the stop and shows the reason (`nodePosReason`) through the same notice a guest's deep link gets (#653). A disabled button says nothing about why. From member up the tap cycles off, positions, reach, off. A tap before `/api/auth/me` answers is held and replayed once the role is known, so a guest never gets a member stop in the URL and a member is never told to log in. A role change repaints the button (`applyObserverGate`).
7. **Node positions is a view choice**, like the layer mode: it no longer counts in the Filters pill and Clear leaves it on (`activeFilterCount`, both copies).
8. **The notices sit at the top centre** on a desktop, between the ticker and the rail: centred, at most 560px, under the bar. They sat at `left: 56px` (52px on a phone) only to clear the old control corner. On a phone they span the width as before. When the ticker is in the way the column moves beside or under it (`noticeplace.js`), so a guest's notice never covers the ticker's cross. That goes past "centred": at 1280 a centred 560px column overlaps the 680px ticker, and a guest notice cannot be dismissed, so it took the clicks on the ticker's close and fold buttons. Kasper kept the moving column after seeing it on 14 September.
9. **The ticker's first visit is top left**, 12px from the left edge under the bar (`tickerplace.js`). It started top right to avoid the zoom control at the top left; that control is gone and the rail owns the right. Below 640px it is pinned under the bar as #643 made it.
10. **The readout and the Locate card stand left of the rail's column** (`--ch-rail-clear`: 14 + 46 + 8px). On a phone the readout keeps the right edge, under the rail's column rather than beside it: the rail stands above it there, so they do not meet, and beside the column the readout wrapped onto two lines at 320px. Kasper chose this over a smaller readout on 14 September. The node-positions notice stops at the column.

## A short screen

A phone held sideways cannot always hold five 46px buttons between the bar and the attribution: at 568x320 there is room for 37px. The buttons shrink to fit, with a 36px floor, and keep 46px wherever it fits, as at 844x390. The size follows the space left under the bar as it renders, so a size near the limit such as 667x375 keeps 46px with one font and shrinks slightly with another. Below 400px of height the gap halves to 4px first. The 36px floor is under the 44px hit-area rule, and Kasper accepted it as an exception on 14 September rather than let the rail reach under the bar or over the attribution, or drop the zoom buttons on a short screen. The app has no such rule because its FABs are not stacked five high on a short screen. On a sideways phone narrower than 641px the pinned ticker and the notices stop at the rail's column instead of spanning the screen, since the column reaches up to the bar there.

`e2e/barlayout.spec.js` hits every rail button at its centre at 375x812, 390x844, 768x1024, 1280x800 and 844x390 as a guest and as a member, and at five sideways sizes down to 568x320.

## Why a cycle button

`docs/design-system.md` asks for a segmented control for three or more states. The rail is the written exception on both surfaces: a FAB is one round target over the map, and a strip of three would not be a rail. The ring shows the stop, which is what the rule protects: the states stay visible, not just inferable from the icon.

## Left out

- **Drag to rotate on the compass.** A press does one thing; turning the map is a right- or ctrl-drag, or two fingers, as before.
- **Tooltips.** The app's FABs and the old view button have none; the labels are for assistive tech.
- **Pitch on the needle.** MapLibre's compass tilted with the pitch; a press here does not change it, so the needle would promise an effect the button does not have.
- **UI for Locate hiding the layer.** While Locate is on, node positions does not draw and the button keeps showing the chosen stop: it is a setting, and it comes back when Locate ends.
- **Zoom out at the world clamp.** MapLibre will not zoom out past a world that fills the map's height, which is above `getMinZoom()` on most screens, so zoom out stays enabled there and does nothing. MapLibre's own control compared against the same number.
