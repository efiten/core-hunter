# The map in 3D, the app's 3D (#595)

**Date:** 2026-09-06
**Status:** decided (Kasper, 2026-09-06), implemented
**Related:** #465 (the map on MapLibre, 2D parity), #147 (the app's 3D), #412 (a bar's tint), #396 (terrain, the same switch on both surfaces), #403 (compass cycle and look-ahead, app only)

## What changed

Since #465 the map runs on MapLibre and `web/mapcore.js` could pitch, but nothing on the site reached it: rotation and tilt were off, there was no `hex-3d`, `points-3d` or `buildings-3d` layer, no terrain and no control. A visitor who knows the app's 3D view could not get the same read of a cell's signal on the site.

Now:

1. **A view button** in the map's control corner under the zoom and the compass, the app's 2D/3D toggle in the house form (a MapLibre control group of one, `mapcore.js addButton`). Pressed, the camera eases to the app's fixed pitch of 60; the hex cells stand as bars in the tint of their own cell (#412), the receptions as octagon pillars collapsed where they coincide (#402), the buildings rise from the hosted style's own vector source, the sky is already there. The layer mode stays the segmented control in the filter panel; which layers a view shows is `maplayers.js`, so Both in 3D draws pillars over flat cells, as in the app.
2. **3D carries the terrain.** The 3D view is the terrain switch: hillshade and the relief mesh come with it, at the exaggeration from Settings, and go with it. Kasper's call (2026-09-06: "3D mode must take terrain and exaggeration along at once"), and the app follows the same rule the same day (#396: its terrain button went). The steps, the DEM source and the plan are `terrain.js`, the app's file.
3. **Rotation and tilt** are on: right- or ctrl-drag, two-finger turn and tilt on touch. MapLibre's own compass draws the bearing and the pitch and puts north back up; it is a mask over `currentColor`, since the library's needle is a dark image on a dark surface.
4. **The view travels in the URL** like `mode` does: `?view=3d` is the layer state and on its own implies the fixed pitch; `?pitch=` and `?bearing=` are the camera and win when present, so a tilt or a turn the visitor chose survives the link. A flat north-up view keeps them out. The exaggeration travels as `?exag=` when off the default, and persists like the theme.

## Shared modules

`signal.js`, `maplayers.js`, `pointmarker.js` and `terrain.js` are the app's files whole in `web/`, pinned byte for byte in `parity.test.js`. The web copy of `signal.js` used to be a hand-kept half; the superset rule says whole modules, never subsets.

## Left out

- The app's compass cycle and look-ahead (#403): they follow the phone's position and heading, which the site does not have.
- A terrain button: the 3D view is the switch, on both surfaces (above).
- Age fade on the pillars: the site's points carry no time window the way the app's records do.
